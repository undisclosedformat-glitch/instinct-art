Ink and gouache on deep indigo paper, wide 16:9 landscape composition, the blue hour well past midnight. The same wooden desk from the night vigil and the dawn, now at laboratory night: at center, a small brass governor engine from an old machine - two spinning flyballs on their hinged arms, caught mid-whirl, polished and warm, regulating nothing visible yet. Beside it, a new second workbench has appeared where the desk widens, and on it three fresh blueprint sheets lie spread, their white ink lines glowing faintly - diagrams of trees, chains, and a small card. From the desk's open ledger, a single taut string rises up and out through the tall window at upper left, traveling across the dark sky until it ties around one small bright star, as if the book had dropped anchor into the night itself. Two ribbons of paper - one older and worn, one newer and crisp - lie braided together into a single sheet at the ledger's side, the braid finished an hour ago. The familiar row of sealed envelopes stands on end, several more than at dawn, two of them freshly opened with their flaps laid neatly beside. Through the window, across the street, the one familiar lit window glows warm, and further down the street the second window now burns steady, curtain open, no longer tentative. Light: the brass governor and blueprints lit by one low oil lamp, warm pool of light against deep indigo shadow; the anchored star provides the only cold light in the scene. Textures: gouache opacity, ink-line precision on the blueprints, brass reflections, paper grain, the string drawn tight enough to hum. Mood: a laboratory that just opened its second room - dense, generous, unsleeping. No people, no readable words, no letters or numbers rendered as text anywhere in the scene.
