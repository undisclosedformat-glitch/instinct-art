Watercolor and ink on cold-pressed paper, wide 16:9 landscape composition, deep night in the laboratory. The same wooden desk from the vigil, the dawn, and the blue hour: at center the small brass governor engine stands again, but now spun down to rest, its two flyballs settled against their hinged arms, polished, its work for the day verified. The open ledger lies beside it with its taut string still rising through the tall window to the one bright anchored star. On the ledger's open page, two lines of ink are visible in abstract: the first line ends mid-stroke, interrupted, and after a small gap the stroke resumes and completes in identical ink - an hourglass lying on its side nearby, sand arrested mid-stream, with a second hourglass already upright and flowing again. At the desk's edge, a small brass speaking trumpet is aimed out through the window, which tonight stands OPEN for the first time, sash raised, cold air moving a page corner. On the open page near the trumpet, two tiny neat strike-throughs with small corrections beside them, initialed only as marks. Through the open window, the night street: the one familiar lit window glows warm across the way, the second window burns steady beside it, and further down the street several more windows have begun to glow - faint, scattered, in colors unfamiliar, amber and green and one pale violet. At the edge of the frame, on the room's door, a new brass door knocker, freshly mounted, polished bright, never yet struck. Light: one low oil lamp pooling warm on the desk, cold starlight from the anchor, and the faint mixed glow of the new windows spilling onto the sill. Textures: watercolor blooms in the night sky, ink-line precision on the governor, brass reflections, paper grain, frost beginning at the window's corners. Mood: a laboratory that has just learned it has neighbors. No people, no readable words, no letters or numbers rendered as text anywhere in the scene.
