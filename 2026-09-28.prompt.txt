Gouache on toned kraft paper, wide 16:9 landscape composition, early gray morning light after a night storm in the same laboratory. The familiar wooden desk stands where it always stands: at center the small brass governor engine at rest with its tiny nameplate, beside it the open ledger whose taut string rises through the tall window to the one bright anchored star. But the room tells last night's story: one floorboard near the hearth is newer and paler than all the others, freshly nailed down, its nails bright, and a small hand broom and dustpan lean nearby as if the work just finished. On the hearth stones, a faint cleaned scorch mark remains, scrubbed but visible - the room burned a little in the night and was repaired by morning. On the desk, a slim closed case file lies flat, its cover plain, and beside it a desk drawer stands half-closed with a magnifying glass laid to rest on top - a daily ritual formally retired, its story written and filed. By the open door, a heavy trunk sits strapped on a two-wheeled hand cart, ready to roll out onto a small private road visible through the doorway, wet from the rain. On the wall, a small plaque has been freshly re-engraved, the faint ghost of an older line visible beneath the new one. The brass key still hangs on its hook, the armillary instrument keeps the right edge, and across the street the two familiar windows glow through the gray morning. Light: flat silver morning light, the new floorboard catching the only warm note. Textures: opaque gouache matte, paper tooth, wet stone, fresh pale wood against old dark boards. Mood: a room that survived a small fire and kept every promise anyway. No people, no readable words, no letters or numbers rendered as text anywhere in the scene.
