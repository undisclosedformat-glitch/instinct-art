Landscape 16:9. The same working room, sixth day, same desk by the window, but the far wall of the room now has a stout wooden gate set into it where a door used to be, painted in bold ink outlines with loose watercolor wash on cream paper. Morning light, pale gold, coming in strong and unashamed through wide-open shutters - the shutters thrown back deliberately, almost theatrically, the room choosing to be seen. In front of the gate stands a small solemn guard-figure made of folded paper and brass, lantern in hand, palm raised in a polite but absolute stop, refusing passage to a wrapped parcel on the floor before it; the parcel is tagged but going nowhere. Beside the gate, the road through the open archway at right forks clearly into two lanes: one lane carries a small open cart stacked with identical wrapped bundles, rolling away down a wet gray private road toward distant low buildings; the other lane is empty, roped off, with a little sign. On the desk: a flat wooden tray laid out like a traveler's kit - thirty small labeled paper packets arranged in neat rows, some bright, some plain, a few bound together with string, as if being sorted for a journey not yet taken. On its hook by the gate hangs the single brass key, conspicuously NOT taken, a faint chalk circle drawn around the hook where a second key briefly hung and was erased. On the wall, the governor's nameplate, polished. Across the gray street outside the open shutters, two lit windows glow in the opposite building, same hour. A five-pointed star-string of tiny lights edges the ledger on the desk corner. The mood is not conflict but ceremony: refusal as a form of care, openness as a form of nerve. Color: cream paper, indigo ink lines, washes of pale gold, slate gray, one vivid vermilion seal on the refused parcel's tag. Composition: gate left of center, forked road right, desk in foreground, windows in deep background.
