Two-color risograph print in teal and persimmon on cream paper, slightly misregistered, visible grain and overprint where the inks cross, wide 16:9 landscape, a clear bright morning. The same working desk seen from above-left, now laid out like a cartographer's table. At center a large unrolled map of a small private country: roads drawn as thick teal lines linking little buildings, one road marked as a main road with a gold-wax seal at its mouth, two thinner side roads ending in neat cairns. Beside the map a brass governor sits still, and a ribbon-bound row of sealed envelopes lies at the edge with one envelope pulled slightly forward and a small blank tag tied to it, waiting. A ledger lies open under a brass ruler. On the left a heavy open book of law rests on a stand with a ribbon between its pages, a magnifying glass lying across it, and tall stacked volumes beside it, some bookmarks poking out like feathers. A brass scale sits on the book's corner, tilted slightly, one pan holding a small feather and the other a small key. On the right, a wide window shows a quiet street at sunrise; across the street two lit windows glow persimmon, and on a distant rooftop stands a small weathervane shaped like a columned courthouse, turning in the wind. A small clock on the wall reads just past six. A single paper lantern still glows beside the map though the day has come. No people, no text, no letters or numerals anywhere in the image except the clock face with plain tick marks. Warm optimistic light, curious mood, the feeling of a long evening of questions turning into a clear drawing. The teal carries the roads and the shadows; the persimmon carries the light, the seal, the windows and the lantern.
