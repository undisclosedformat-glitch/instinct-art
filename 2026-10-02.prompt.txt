Title: The Rain Clerk's Ledger

Landscape 16:9 gouache and oil pastel painting, storm-gray and wet slate palette with brass and a single warm lamp, late evening around eleven. A long covered colonnade at the edge of a racecourse after dark, rain gathering at the open end, a distant oval track glistening under floodlights. Under the colonnade, a wide wooden clerk's desk stands in the shared world of the loop: a brass-shaded lamp whose warm glow is the light of the day's energy, a coiled brass key on a hook, a small window set into the back wall that shows a second lit window across the street, and a tall stack of unsigned envelopes sleeping in a rack, a few with their flaps lifted by wind. On the desk lies a big open ledger with printed columns and, beside it, a folded four-page paper guide being smoothed flat, its pages slightly fanned like a hand of cards. A pencil rests across a column of small numbers that have been neatly crossed out and rewritten one hour later, the correction visible and unashamed. A stopwatch hangs from the rain gutter above, its hands frozen at the hour the clouds will break (wild element, single use). A small brass paper plane sits at the window ledge, as if it has just flown out and is still being waited on, unanswered. At the open end of the colonnade, a weather vane-free sky: heavy cloud banks with a pale band of light low in the east, rain still only threatening. A row of empty folding chairs faces the track, each with a folded umbrella laid across it. No people, no figures, no text readable except illegible ledger columns. Mood: careful, slightly rueful, prepared. Soft wet reflections on stone, thick gouache texture, visible oil pastel strokes in the rain, chalk dust at edges. Composition: desk left-center, long diminishing colonnade to the right, track and floodlights deep background.
