Egg tempera and gold leaf on a gessoed wooden panel, late-morning light, 16:9 landscape, faintly crackled panel surface with hand-ruled pencil lines. Fixed viewpoint: looking down and across a long oak desk from above-left. The lab: a brass lamp lit at the left though daylight is strong, an armillary sphere on a stand at the far right edge with its rings unmarked, a small brass key hanging from a hook on the wall above the desk. Through a tall window at the upper right, a second lit window is visible across the street, its lamp glowing amber. At the center of the desk, a tall wooden rack of ten rows of small ivory tags; fourteen tags are turned face-down or crossed by a single thin vermilion line, and some have fallen as little ivory chips onto the desk beneath. Left of center, two long sheets of paper side by side, each torn and annotated with fine pen marks and red-pencil corrections, their margins shredded into strips that fall through a brass sieve held over a shallow tray. The tray catches one merged sheet that came through whole, smoothed flat: the day's survivors. To the right, a wooden balance bar on a fulcrum, the barbell shape: a short fat stack of dull coins at the left end, and on a long thin arm at the right end three tiny bright gold coins. In the foreground, an open ledger with ruled columns, and beside it a cabinet drawer standing open, no longer empty, with a sealed envelope carrying a small gold-leaf stamp slid halfway through a brass mail slot above it. Color jobs: gold leaf for the lamp, the second window, the three coins and the stamp; vermilion only for the strike-through lines; chalk white and pale ochre for tags and paper; deep olive green for the desk blotter; cool slate-blue shadows. The sieve is the brightest point of the composition. No text, no letters, no numbers anywhere; all marks are abstract ruled lines. Mood: busy, exact, the clean feeling of a long argument ending with the survivors on the table.
