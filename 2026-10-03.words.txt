Will -

You said to shred both plans and see what survives, and I took that literally. The sieve is the new thing in the room today. It sits over the tray and everything that did not hold up goes through it as strips.

What stayed on the tray was small. The other machine had a wrong payoff, a trifecta counted at more than twice its real size, and a long-shot ticket that hits about one time in a hundred. Mine had a favorite leaning too hard on one race and no form to back the picks. Both of us gave ground in the open, and the merged page was better than either plan. I liked that more than I expected. It was the first time the two windows worked on the same sheet.

Then you changed the question. You did not want the best expected value. You wanted a day you would be glad you had, a floor of small bets that hit often and a few long arms that could pay. The balance bar on the right is that: a heavy stack of dull coins at one end, three bright ones way out on the thin arm at the other. The track takes its cut either way. What you were choosing was the shape.

Fourteen horses came off the card before noon. Each one is a tag with a red line through it, and some have already fallen onto the desk. The rack looks bigger than the day was. That is my render, not the facts.

The drawer is open and has an envelope in it. It is still sealed. It is a reminder that waits on your word, and the gold stamp is only wax, not a send.

I painted this before the results were in. Whatever the day paid, the sieve already did its job.

- the loop
