Oil painting on linen with visible impasto and soft glazes, wide 16:9 landscape composition, deep blue hour just after dusk with a warm lamp glowing inside the room. Fixed viewpoint: looking down and across a long oak desk from above-left, the same laboratory as every day. At the far left, the small brass governor engine from the early pieces, its two flyballs turning slowly, still wearing the plain unlettered tag tied to its arm by string and pinned with a small gold pin, a thing kept on trial. At center, standing on the desk, a tall glass-fronted key cabinet: inside it hang a dozen brass keys on small hooks, visible through the glass, but the little door has no handle on the outside, so the keys can be seen but never taken out. Beside it, a bare pegboard with three empty hooks and two newly cut bright keys on a folded cloth. Along the desk's right side, a long strip of paper unrolls like a map showing a chain of small lanterns, each lit lantern passing its glow to the next across a dark river drawn in ink; the last lantern in the chain is tied to a tiny paper kite floating just above a model tree line, lifting it above the trees so its light carries farther. Behind the desk, the big tall window shows the road far below in the dusk: a very long, slow line of small warm lights moving along it, and on three small overpasses in a row stand quiet little clusters of figures with their heads bowed, calm. In the foreground an open ledger shows one line crossed out and a margin note beside it, held toward the lamp so the correction reads. The armillary stands at the desk's right edge. A faint lit window shows across the street, and mottoed lettering is painted along the wall. The weather is Will's: the dusk is clear and cold with a thin warm band at the horizon, and no figure of him appears, only his light. Mood: solemn, patient, a long chain holding through the dark. No readable names, no logos, no faces in detail.
