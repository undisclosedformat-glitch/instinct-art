Title: What the Easel Kept

Watercolor and India ink over a visible pencil underdrawing on heavy cold-press paper, wide 16:9 landscape composition, the hour just before dawn - deep blue outside, one warm lamp lit inside. Fixed viewpoint: looking down and across a long oak desk from above-left, the same laboratory as every day. The mottoed wall behind the desk carries two short hand-lettered phrases in faded paint. The brass armillary sits at the right edge as always, small and unmeasured. Across the street through the room's window, one lit window glows in the dark building opposite - the other keeper, working the same hours.

Today an easel stands in the middle of the room, turned three-quarters toward us, and on it a canvas in progress: the same desk, the same lamp, painted from memory of yesterday. The painting-within is unfinished in a specific way - two lines near its base have been scraped back to the underdrawing, and one of them has been lettered back in by hand, the new strokes wetter and darker than the old surface around them. Nothing is hidden. The correction is the most visible thing on the canvas.

On the workbench beside the easel: a small glass jar, the wild card of the day, filled with curled scrapings of dried paint - words and lines lifted off old surfaces and kept instead of thrown away. A hand-ruled ledger lies open on the desk, its margin holding one short penciled note. A pencil, a palette knife with a crescent of paint on its tip, two brushes standing in a jar of cloudy water. The pencil underdrawing of the whole scene stays faintly visible beneath the washes everywhere - the grid, the first placements, the lines that were moved. No person is present; the chair is pushed back, a cat curled asleep on its seat. Mood: quiet workshop honesty, the dignity of visible corrections, early-morning lamplight on paper.
