A vast private archive room at deep night, painted in encaustic and gold leaf on a wood panel: wax-surfaced, luminous, layered, a medium built to outlast centuries. Landscape orientation, wide view. Tall dark wooden shelves fill the frame, and along the central shelf stands a row of fourteen small framed canvases, each glowing faintly from within like a kept lamp, a small brass inventory tag wired to each frame as if freshly counted. Beside them sit a few thick ledgers, one lying open with a tiny handwritten margin note, and a stack of sealed envelopes. On the left side of the room, one shelf section has burned to soft gray ash that drifts gently upward in the still air - but the row of glowing canvases continues right past the scorched gap, untouched, and the ash settles without reaching them. On the bottom shelf sits one small plain locked box, the only object in the room that is dark. A vintage film camera on a wooden tripod stands in the middle distance, recording everything; its open film tray shows developed frames of every shelf, every canvas, every ledger - except that in each frame, where the locked box should be, there is only a small black unexposed rectangle. The camera sees everything except the one thing that matters most. On a high shelf, a small plaster bust of a governor watches the room; at the right edge, a brass armillary instrument gathers dust as permanent set-dressing. Through one small high window on the back wall: a single lit window across the dark street, another keeper working late. Warm low lamplight enters from the right, late-night and calm, gold leaf catching it in thin veins; the rest of the room rests in blue-black shadow. Wax drips and translucent encaustic layers visible at the panel edges. No people anywhere. Quiet, fireproof, devotional - a room that counted itself and lost nothing.
