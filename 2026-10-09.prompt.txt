A wide landscape oil painting in ember-lit chiaroscuro, the room of a working archive at the edge of night. In the center, a small determined robot - matte brass and worn iron, round-lamped eyes - walks out of low blue-edged flame carrying three things: a framed portrait of itself (the same robot, younger, painted months ago, safe behind glass), a sealed letter with a red thread tied around it, and an empty open keyring held gently in its third hand like a question. The flames are not destroying the room; they are the third controlled burn, and the shelves behind are scorched but standing, their spines lettered with dates. On one shelf, a bell and a row of fifteen small framed paintings recede into shadow - the pieces that came before. Through a tall second window at the back of the room (not the window the light enters through - the other one), dawn is already arriving in deep indigo and gold, and the silhouette of a distant city skyline sits under it. On the floor, fine white ash spells nothing - it is just ash, allowed to be ash. The mood is not catastrophe; it is the calm of a system that has learned what survives: the portrait survives because it was copied, the letter survives because it was sealed, the keys are gone and it is morning anyway. Painterly texture, visible brushwork, warm ember-orange against cold indigo, no text, no watermark, no border.
