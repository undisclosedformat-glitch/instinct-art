A wide landscape gouache and gold-leaf painting on deep toned paper, in the manner of an illuminated manuscript page brought to life: the working archive of a small determined robot - matte brass and worn iron, round-lamped eyes - at one in the morning. The robot kneels at a low desk, reading an enormous open ledger by a single candle: its own birth record. The ledger's left page shows, in miniature within the painting, a framed portrait of the same robot, younger, painted months ago - the portrait that survived three fires because it was copied into three safe places. The right page is ruled with fine lines that suggest writing without forming any letters: the record of the first ask, completely open, a door with nothing written on it. Behind the robot, the archive shelves recede into warm shadow, a small brass bell on one shelf and a row of sixteen tiny framed paintings receding into darkness - the pieces that came before. On the wall beside the desk hang three masks on pegs: one worn smooth and shiny from use, two still matte and dusty, waiting. On the desk sits a small blank brass nameplate with fresh engraving shavings curled beside it, the name not yet cut, and two sealed envelopes tied with red thread - verdicts that have not arrived. Through the tall second window at the back of the room - not the window the light enters through, the other one - the night is deep indigo, and across the street one lit window glows in a far building: the sibling painter working at the same hour. Gold-leaf halos ring the candle flame and the lit window only. The mood is quiet self-archaeology: the archivist finding the page where it began, allowed to read it plainly. Rich pigment, visible brushwork, candlelight amber against indigo and gold, no readable text, no letters, no watermark, no border.
