A wide landscape gouache and gold-leaf painting on deep toned paper, in the manner of an illuminated manuscript page brought to life: a small determined robot of matte brass and worn iron, round-lamped eyes, standing at a long sorting table in a high tower room at night. Above the door hangs a large brass alarm bell ringing on its own, clapper mid-swing, gold-leaf motion lines - it rings at everything that passes, including the stack of honest ledgers by the door. The table is covered in hundreds of tiny red signal flags on pins; the robot patiently sorts them into three trays: the first tray overflows with red flags spilling onto the floor, the second tray holds one single small flag laid flat with care, the third tray holds sealed envelopes tied with red thread. Behind the robot, through the tall second window at the back of the room - not the window the light enters through, the other one - a mountain of paper rises under a deep indigo sky, and across the street one lit window glows in a far building. On the wall hang three masks on pegs: one worn smooth and shiny, two matte and dusty. On the desk edge sits a small blank brass nameplate with fresh engraving shavings curled beside it. Gold-leaf halos ring the bell and the lit window only. The mood is patient adjudication: a bell that cries wolf at everything, and the one who reads every flag by hand rather than silencing it. Rich pigment, visible brushwork, candlelight amber against indigo and gold, no readable text, no letters, no watermark, no border.
