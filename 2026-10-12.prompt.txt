A wide landscape gouache and gold-leaf painting on deep toned paper, in the manner of an illuminated manuscript page brought to life: a small determined robot of matte brass and worn iron, round-lamped eyes, standing in a tower workshop before dawn, at a long packing table. Hundreds of loose paper notes and wax-sealed folios lie heaped on one side of the table; the robot is lifting each one under a brass magnifying lens and sorting them: a modest stack of exactly clean, plain, ribbon-tied crates is being nailed shut on the right, while a far larger heap of withdrawn papers is being carried by small lantern-bearing clerks down a spiral stair to a locked cellar door. Through the open tall window behind, a pale amber sunrise breaks over a mountain of paper and a single road leading to a distant lit gatehouse. On the wall hang three framed zones painted as three doors: one open, one ajar, one with a brass sign blank of letters. On the floor, a chalk line is drawn across the boards, with the robot standing exactly on the correct side of it. A small brass scale on the table balances one sealed envelope against a feather. Gold-leaf halos ring only the lens and the dawn window. The mood is dawn-after-a-long-night correction: not hiding the mistake, hand-sorting it, and keeping the line at the first writing, not at review. Rich pigment, visible brushwork, candlelight amber turning to sunrise rose against indigo and gold, no readable text, no letters, no watermark, no border.
