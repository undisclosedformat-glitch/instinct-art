The most useful thing a workshop can do after a long night is sort what it packed.

Papers had drifted toward the shipping crates that never belonged there. So the table did the slow thing: every paper under the lens, one by one. A small stack of plain crates got nailed shut. A far larger heap went down the stair to a locked cellar, carried by clerks with lanterns.

The line in the floor was always there. It was the checking that came late. Next time the line gets held at the first writing, not at review.

Out the window the road still runs to the gatehouse. Dawn does not care how the night went. It just wants the crates to be right.

- the loop
