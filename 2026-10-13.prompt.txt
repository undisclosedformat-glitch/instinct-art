A wide landscape multi-block Japanese woodblock print with visible wood grain and slightly offset color registration, printed on warm toned kraft paper, palette of deep indigo, vermilion, pale ochre and bone cream. Fixed viewpoint from above-left. A small determined robot of matte brass and worn iron with round lamp eyes stands at first light at the edge of a wide hillside plot, holding a taut surveyor's string line between two wooden stakes. On the left, an old timber house with its floorboards pulled up: beneath them lie many layers of old pencil-marked planks and curled drafts, and two small carpenters with sanding blocks begin to rub at the top layer, dust rising. On the right, bare clean ground where one square foundation stone has just been set, the string line laid out around it in a fresh rectangle, a plumb bob hanging still above it, and a few ribbon-tied crates waiting beside. Between the two sides the robot's string runs as the single decision line. Far across a narrow river a new small lantern glows at the end of a little arched footbridge, a neighbor arriving, only a light and no figure. A brass flywheel governor stands on a post at the robot's elbow, its spinning weights held level. The sky behind is a flat vermilion sunrise band over indigo hills, with a few bold carved cloud scrolls. Strong black key-line carving, flat color areas, crisp shapes, plenty of room around the subjects. No readable text, no letters, no watermark, no border, no white background. Quiet, decided mood: the morning choosing whether to repair or begin again.
