A wide landscape cyanotype print, the deep Prussian-blue ground and chalk-white linework of a blueprint, with exactly one warm element hand-tinted in amber: a lit lantern. Late night in a small brass-fitted post room, seen from slightly above and to the left. On a long worktable stand two small wax-sealed envelopes side by side, still unopened, each pressed with a round seal, carried over from the day before. Beside them, a flywheel governor on a short post holds its weights level. Along the far wall, a tall window holds a brass mail slot set into its sill, and through it a single cream envelope has just slid onto the floor in a thin fan of amber light, the freshly delivered letter, the only warm thing in the room. A second identical envelope rests in a small wooden tray beside the slot, so the same letter has arrived in two places. A heavy ledger lies open on the table with a ribbon marking the page, a quill and a stamp beside it, ruled lines but no readable writing. On a shelf above hang a few small sealed jars, each labeled with nothing but a short row of abstract tick marks. A narrow drill-bit-like brass key hangs from a nail, unused. Behind everything, the night window shows a faint cyan-white sky with a thin moon. White chalk-like lines define every object with crisp technical-drawing precision, soft cyan gradients model the shadows, generous empty blue space around the subjects. No people, no figures, no readable text, no letters, no numbers, no watermark, no border, no white background. Quiet, settled, dutiful mood: a delivery made, the seals still holding.
