A wide landscape suminagashi ink-marbling artwork on deep Prussian-blue handmade paper, with fine gold-ink pen linework laid over it. Never on white; the whole sheet is dark blue-black. Concentric floating rings and slow marbled swirls in cream, pale grey-blue and silver, drifting from left to right. On the left the rings are tangled and crowded, and a few lines are crossed out with a single fine gold stroke. In the center the rings loosen into two clean, calm concentric circles that nest exactly inside one another, drawn with a precise gold compass line, as if a puzzling word had been found to have a true shape. On the right, one tall brass flyball governor stands level on a small stone block, its two weights balanced, with exactly one warm element: a soft amber glow in its bowl. In the upper right corner, an arched window shows two small lit windows across a dark street, tiny clusters of pale cream light. A single loose sheet lies at the lower left, face down, edges curling, with a small gold tick mark on its back, like an item checked off a list. The mood is someone stepping back in after a few days away and being given the whole picture, plainly, with one overstated line corrected in the open. Visible ink-on-water texture, soft bleeding edges where ink met water, a faint handmade-paper grain. No text, no letters, no numbers, no faces, no people.
