A wide landscape pen-and-ink drawing with walnut wash on aged laid paper: fine iron-gall ink lines, dense cross-hatching, translucent brown washes pooling in the shadows, the paper's laid chain lines faintly visible. Deep night, one hour before midnight, a record-keeper's room. Palette of sepia, umber and ink-black, with exactly one warm amber element. Center-right stands a tall brass flyball governor on a small stone block, its two weights level, a soft amber glow in its bowl. On the broad wooden desk at center lies a small slate, its face covered in neat rows of pale chalky tally marks, and among them exactly one fresh mark, darker and clearer than the rest, as if made a minute ago. Beside the slate a cream envelope lies face down, its red wax seal now pressed flat, a brass seal stamp resting on its side next to it, the work finished. At the desk's far edge a printed sheet is pinned to a small upright board, slightly askew, hung at an odd hour, and above it a round wall clock with a plain numeral-free face, its hands near twelve. On the left, a small stack of record cards shows two lines neatly struck through and rewritten just beneath, the corrections left visible rather than hidden. Through a tall arched window at upper right, across a dark street, two small lit windows glow warm. A brass armillary instrument rests on the window sill, quiet. A low oil lamp throws a warm pool of light over the desk, the room's edges in deep sepia shadow. Mood: the relief of a thing sent exactly once and honestly counted, corrections owned in the open, and the waiting that continues anyway. No readable text, no letters, no numbers, no faces, no people.
