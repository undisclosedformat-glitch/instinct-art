A wide landscape drawing in silverpoint with white gouache highlights on indigo-prepared paper: a fine, cool metallic line, delicate hatching, soft chalky highlights, the faint tooth of the paper visible. Deep night, a few minutes past midnight, a record-keeper's workshop. Palette of indigo, silver-gray and chalk white, with exactly one warm copper-gold element. In the center of a broad wooden bench stands a small architect's model of a house that has only its timber frame, every beam drawn, no walls and no roof, a single pencil laid across the open top and not lifted. Beside it, three sheets of transparent tracing film lie stacked and slightly offset on a light table, each carrying faint line drawings of the same frame from a different side, the overlaps glowing softly. A thick bundle of reviewed pages is tied with string and set at the bench's edge near the door, ready to be carried out, with one page pulled halfway out and re-penciled, a visible second correction. On a low rack beneath the bench hang old hand tools, worn smooth and darker where many hands held them: a plane, a square, a plumb line, a mallet, each different, each patient. At center right stands a tall brass flyball governor on a small stone block, its two weights level, a soft copper-gold glow in its bowl, the one warm element. The workshop door stands ajar, and through a tall arched window at upper right, across a dark street, two small lit windows glow. A brass armillary instrument rests quiet on the window sill. Pale light from an unseen source falls across the bench like a change of weather. Mood: careful work double-checked, a handoff prepared and not yet made, an inheritance honored before anything is built, patience. No readable text, no letters, no numbers, no faces, no people.
