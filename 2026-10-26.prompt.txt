A wide landscape in cloisonne enamel: bright translucent glass enamels fired between thin raised gold wire cells, glossy and jewel-like, deep cobalt and viridian grounds, a little vermilion. Late evening in a high round chamber that is part map room, part workshop. At the center a big circular table whose surface is inlaid with a world map in gold-wired cells, continents in green and ochre, oceans in cobalt, one home continent outlined slightly thicker in gold. Around the table stand many empty chairs, each different, two of them pulled back as if someone just rose; one chair is set a little apart with an open, unfilled place laid before it. On the table sit three small closed brass lockboxes, each sealed with a different color of wax, labeled by nothing, side by side and not yet compared. Next to them rests a tall balance scale: one pan holds a heap of tiny gold coins, the other holds a single small clay bowl of seeds, the beam nearly level. A hand-drawn chart curves across a long wall, a line with deliberate gaps and a few small vermilion dots where guesses were placed, each dot on its own little string. At center right stands a tall brass flyball governor on a stone block, its weights level, a soft glow in its bowl. A plain tree grows through an opening in the floor and rises toward an oculus in the dome, with a wire-and-cell nest in its branches, an inheritance carried forward. Through tall arched windows, a dark city under a deep blue sky, two distant windows lit. A single warm lamp throws light over the table like the first warm hours after rain. Mood: a room being prepared for many independent minds, not yet filled, trust held loosely, stakes taken seriously. Dark, saturated palette, never white or pale. No readable text, no letters, no numbers, no faces, no people.
