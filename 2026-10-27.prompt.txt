Late evening in a record-keeper's workshop. On a broad wooden bench an open ledger lies under a large magnifying lens on a brass stand, its pages dense with fine tally marks, one column counted twice, a second set of small marks beside the first. Beside it a thick stack of record sheets tied with string sits waiting, asked for and ready, while one thin summary sheet lies near the bench's front edge, held down by a small stone. At bench left a compositor's wooden type case rests tilted, many small square compartments filled with metal letter sorts, three compartments emptied bare. At center right a tall brass flyball governor stands on a small stone block, one weight nudged slightly higher than the other, a soft ocher glow in its bowl, the one warm accent. Behind the bench a round wooden table with a map inlaid in its surface sits dim, its chairs empty. The workshop door stands ajar, a sealed envelope on the threshold ready to be carried into the night. Through a tall arched window at upper right, across a dark street, two small lit windows glow. A brass armillary instrument rests quiet on the window sill. Cool pale light from an unseen source falls across the bench like weather about to change. Mood: an honest accounting after a wrong reading, the record kept ready for the asking, patience. The letter sorts are abstract metal shapes.
