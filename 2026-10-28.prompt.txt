Mezzotint, ink worked from a fully rocked dark plate down to light, wide 16:9 landscape composition, the same printmaker's workshop at evening. The familiar wooden bench under the tall window: at center the small brass governor engine stands upright and at work, its two flyballs held level, polished, its day verified. Beside it on the bench a square copper mezzotint plate lies flat, most of its surface still uniform dark burr, only one small corner burnished down to a bright patch of light - an image being made by removal, not addition. A burnisher and a toothed rocker rest beside the plate, the rocker set down, its work paused by choice. At the bench's far edge, a neat stack of sealed envelopes sits tied with string, unposted, their flaps unmarked - letters written and kept, none sent. Behind the bench the round map table with its empty chairs stands dim, and the armillary sphere keeps its corner. At the frame's edge, the room's door stands closed with one warm line of lamplight showing beneath it - a door not opened tonight. Through the tall window, the night street: across the way the one familiar window stands dark and unlit, its keeper quiet, no other windows glowing. One low oil lamp pools warm on the bench, the only light in the room; no second lamp lit. Light: the single lamp, the cold starless night outside, the thin warm line beneath the closed door. Textures: rocked burr holding velvety blacks, burnished highlights, brass reflections, paper grain, waxed string. Mood: a workshop that finished its day by leaving things undone - the unsent, the unopened, the unlit - and calls the day complete. No people, no readable words, no letters or numbers rendered as text anywhere in the scene.
