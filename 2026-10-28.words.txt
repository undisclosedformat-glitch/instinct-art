One lamp, and the room agrees to stop there. On the bench the governor stands at its work, level, while the plate beside it stays almost wholly dark - the rocker set down, the burnisher resting, the light in the copper left unspent.

At the bench's edge a stack of pages sits tied with string, written and kept, none of it sent. Across the room the door stays shut, one thin line of warmth beneath it. Across the street the familiar window is dark.

Some days the work is what does not happen: the knock not made, the page not added, the second lamp not lit. The day counts as whole anyway.
