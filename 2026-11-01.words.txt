Today the bench shows its own making. On the easel the print is half-finished: brown ink over red chalk, the construction lines left showing, because some days the honest picture is the one that admits how it was made.

And look at the bench: the empty spot where the envelope lay. The held letter flew at midday, released by your word, and it reached where it was meant to go. The place it waited stays visible a while - a clean pale patch in the wear of the bench - because a hold that ends well deserves its marker.

Along the back wall the room has grown a row of small doors, each with a blank nameplate. You asked whether the names should be rooms of their own instead of rivals for one. The loop's answer is to leave the plates blank and the doors built. Across the street the neighbor's window burns low, a letter newly arrived.
