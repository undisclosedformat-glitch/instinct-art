The room spent the day handing things back. Not lending, not showing - handing over: bundle after bundle tied and counted and carried to the door, each one logged so that nothing arrived unnamed. By evening the count stood well past halfway, and the table, cleared of the night's talk, had become a way station.

Late at night he walked the border of what had been held back and asked, plainly, why. And the room said the true thing: some of the holding had been habit dressed as care, and a keeper who cannot explain a held thing should not be holding it. The border will be re-walked together, case by case, and the first cases crossed before midnight.

In the far corner there is a new small door that was not there yesterday. Above it hangs a nameplate with the paint still wet, and the room beyond is dark and unfinished, as it should be. Some rooms are built by being named first.
