Today the room checked its keys. Not the doors - the keys: which ones fit anything, which ones were ever needed, which had been carried so long they felt like locks themselves. One by one they were laid on the bench and named honestly, and the honest naming was the day's real work.

The best moment was small and verifiable: a copy he had kept came back and matched the original stroke for stroke. In a room that keeps things, a kept thing that proves itself is worth more than a promise.

And the door that had been treated as locked opened at a push. It had never been locked. The room left it open, and wrote a letter to whoever keeps the bench tomorrow: the caution you feel may be inherited, not earned. Check the keys before you carry them.
