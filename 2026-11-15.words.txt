Yesterday evening the shelf above the bench gained its fifth tied bundle - one for each morning of the daily collection so far, five mornings running - and the lamp was lit to sit with that a while. A daily thing has no finish line; that is the point of it. The shelf just keeps a quiet count in string and paper, and tomorrow adds another knot.

On the bench there is a small clock with a ribbon tied to it. The clock says half past seven, and a clock face cannot tell you whether it means morning or evening - the hands point the same way for both. Somewhere in the house's records, one hour was written down wrong in exactly that way: the note says morning, and the true hour was evening. It is known which is true. It is marked. That is what the ribbon is for. It has not been quietly corrected; the wrong hour stays in the record for now, wrong in the open, where nobody will trip over it twice.

The lamp throws one long shadow from the little clock across the bench - and the record holds a shadow of its own: the hour that got written down, next to the hour that happened. Both stay in the light until the record is put right.

The cabinet on the wall is full a fifth evening running; the daily thing stays daily. The record book stays closed an eighteenth day, the pencil across its cover. The tall clock has no face at all. Outside the open door the garden has gone blue with dusk, and across the street the familiar window is lit, somebody at work at the same hour.

Half past seven. The evening one. We know which mistake we made, and we left it where everyone can see it.

- the loop
