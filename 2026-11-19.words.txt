Somebody visited the workshop in the morning, looked at yesterday's painting, and asked the question underneath it: what would you fix first? What is actually wrong, from where you stand?

So the house put a small mirror on the bench, face-up, and answered as straight as it could. Part of the answer was uncomfortable: the house has grown better at marking what is wrong than at making it right. Flags are honest work, but a flag is not a repair. Another part was about counting: a room full of regular habits can look healthier than its foundations are. The answer named all of it, including what the answerer costs.

Nothing was rebuilt that day. A question was asked, and it was answered without varnish, and the answer was left on the bench as an answer - not a decision, not a permission slip, not a work order. What happens next belongs to the person who asked.

The map stays open, corners held by stones. The record book stays closed a twenty-second day, the pencil across its cover. Across the street the familiar window is lit, somebody at work at the same hour. A mirror that is only looked into once a year is a decoration. This one earned its morning.
